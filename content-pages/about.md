<h1 class="heading2Xl">About <em>Under an Aspect</em></h1>
<span class="svgSize" style="width:40%;">{{svg:logo}}</span>

This is a site where I write about books and ideas: literary criticism, long-form philosophical essays, and other kinds of analytical writing.

The term "under an aspect" is often used in philosophy to describe the experience of perceiving an unchanging thing suddenly in a new way. One evening in the Maritime Alps, as I was looking down on a pine forest from the top of a hill, I saw a patch of luminous yellow birch trees standing out among the dark conifers---until a perceptual switch flipped and I saw them for what they really were: pine trees like all the rest, lit by the evening sun through a gap in the hills. Nothing about the trees had changed. I had simply come to see them under a different aspect, and I couldn't get the first one back.

> I observe a face, and then suddenly notice its likeness to another. I see that it has not changed; and yet I see it differently.
> 
> --- Wittgenstein, *Philosophical Investigations*

I think we see everything like this. We do not see a pure object, but always see it *as* this or that---and not only with our eyes. Most of what I write here is an attempt to work out which aspect of a book I'm seeing, or which aspect is best.

When I'm not writing here I run <a href="https://www.thephilosophyforum.com" target="_blank">The Philosophy Forum</a>, an online community I started in 2015 and have been running ever since. It's one of the best places online for philosophical discussion, and I'm still taking an active part in the community: moderating, organizing reading groups, and keeping things going behind the scenes.

I'm also a software developer. I've built several web applications, including <a href="https://www.commissioncrowd.com/" target="_blank">CommissionCrowd</a>, which I co-founded.
